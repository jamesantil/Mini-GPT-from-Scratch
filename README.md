Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT have revolutionized natural language pro
cessing, primarily due to the power of the Transformer architecture. In this project, we
explore these foundations by building a character-level language model from the ground
up. Thedevelopment process was iterative: we began with a fundamental Bigram baseline
to establish a simple probabilistic standard, then progressively upgraded the architecture
into a sophisticated, GPT-style decoder-only Transformer utilizing masked self-attention.
This report documents the complete lifecycle of the project, detailing our learning out
comes, architectural implementation, and experimental framework. We also discuss spe
cific debugging challenges encountered during training and analyze the final generation
quality. To conclude, we present a comparative evaluation of the Bigram baseline against
the final Transformer model, highlighting significant differences in cross-entropy loss,
syntactic coherence, and the ability to capture long-range context
